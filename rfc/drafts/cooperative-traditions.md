Concordance should feel like a natural extension of the world's long-lived cooperative practices, not a replacement for them. Four design consequences follow.

**New accords (after v0)**

- **Work day** (after gotong royong, minga, dugnad): a task, a date, a shared meal, an open invitation.
- **Farewell and grief** (after burial societies and iddir): meals, visits and practical help for a member's family through illness or death; private by default and guided by the family's wishes.
- **Open dispute hour** (after the Valencia Water Tribunal): a regular, short time when anyone can bring a disagreement to be heard quickly and simply.

**Guarding against "us versus them"**

- No comparisons or rankings between circles.
- No shared blocklists between circles beyond genuine safety concerns.
- Federation makes helping other circles easy, so the natural movement is outward.

**Local names and forms.** Circles name their practices in their own words, and the accord library holds versions contributed by circles in different cultures.

**Designed for renewal.** A quiet circle can be revived from its records; experienced members can take a mentoring role for new circles; a shared archive keeps each circle's stories when its founders move on.

**Bahá'í practice.** Bahá'í consultation seeks the truth together rather than winning an argument, and Bahá'í elections have no candidates or campaigning. Bahá'u'lláh's call for a universal auxiliary language, taught beside every mother tongue, is one inspiration for Selewa.
