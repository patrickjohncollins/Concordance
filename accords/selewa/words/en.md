# Selewa Seed

## What this is

Selewa is Concordance's own language, and *Selewa* is also Concordance's name for itself in that language. It means "hearts together" (*sele*, heart; *wa*, together), as Concordance does from the Latin. In English text we say Concordance, and we write Selewa only when speaking of the language itself or quoting it.

This is the seed: stage 0 of the bootstrap, written in English. It holds the sounds, a one-page grammar, about a hundred roots drawn from the Charter, and a first rendering of the Charter's opening. Everything here is a proposal for circles to try, change and adopt; nothing is final until it is taken into use.

Selewa is an auxiliary language. It sits beside every mother tongue and replaces none.

## Sounds

**Vowels (5):** a, e, i, o, u, as in most of the world's languages: *a* as in "father," *e* as in "bed," *i* as in "machine," *o* as in "more," *u* as in "rule."

**Consonants (10):** p, t, k, m, n, s, l, w, y, h. Nearly every human language has sounds close to these.

**Syllables:** a vowel, optionally with one consonant before it, and optionally *n* after it. So *a*, *ka*, *an* and *kan* are all syllables. No consonant clusters, and no word ends in a consonant other than *n*.

**Stress:** always on the first syllable of a root.

**What every voice keeps.** The core fixes which sounds must stay *distinct*, not exactly how they are made. Each regional voice may pronounce them its own way, as long as all fifteen stay different from each other:

- *p, t, k* may be voiced (b, d, g) or not.
- *l* may be l, r or a tapped r.
- *s* may be s or sh; *w* may be w or v; *h* may be h or a soft kh.
- A final *n* may be a full n or just a nasal vowel.

This is what lets Selewa be spoken with a Cairo accent, a Seoul accent or a Lima accent and still be the same language.

## Grammar on one page

1. **Roots never change.** No endings, no irregular forms, no grammatical gender.
2. **Any root can be a thing, an act or a quality**, depending on where it stands. *Lo* is "give" and also "a gift"; *tan* is "good" and "to be good."
3. **The modifier comes first, the head last.** *Piti kulu* is a small circle. Joined roots make new words the same way: *su* (fire) + *hu* (person) = *suhu*, "those who share the fire."
4. **Plain order is doer, act, thing.** *Mi lo o hamupo*: I give food.
5. **Roles can be marked, so any order is possible.** *ka* marks the doer, *o* the thing acted on, *pe* the one it goes to or is for, *lu* what it is done with, *ti* where or when. With the marks, *O hamupo ka mi lo* means the same as *Mi lo o hamupo*. Each voice may favor the order that sounds natural to it.
6. **Longer descriptions follow the noun after *li* ("that, which").** *Kulu li pan hu aki wenu*: a circle that anyone can join.
7. **Little is required, much is available.** Time is unmarked unless it matters: *pa* (already), *si* (now), *wi* (later) go before the act. *Wa* after a noun means "together, more than one": *hu* person, *huwa* people.
8. **Two words for "we."** *Mini* is "we, including you." *Mika* is "we, not including you." The Charter speaks in *mini*.
9. **Saying no.** *Ne* goes before what it denies: *ne toka*, not sold.
10. **Asking.** *Ho* at the end makes a question: *Ni na, ho?* Do you need something? Question words join *ho* to a root: *hohu* who, *hopo* what, *hoti* where.
11. **Being.** *Ye* links two things that are the same: *Selewa ye sele wa*, Selewa is hearts together.
12. **Joining.** *e* (and), *ke* (if), *yan* (because).

**Grammar words:** e, o, ka, pe, lu, ti, li, ye, ke, yan, ho, ne, pa, si, wi, wa. Sixteen in all.

## The Charter's opening in Selewa

A first attempt at stage 1 of the bootstrap: the Charter's opening, written with only the roots and grammar above. Each line shows the Selewa, then word by word, then the English it renders.

**1. The name**

> *Selewa ye sele wa.*
>
> Selewa · is · heart · together
>
> Concordance is hearts together.

**2. What it is**

> *Selewa ye posapo li huwa aso wa: mini hapa o po li mini hon, mini tika tenu o kipi po, e mini tenu wa, ti piti kulu li pan hu aki wenu e pan hu aki kosa.*
>
> Selewa · is · way · that · people · help · together: we · share · (thing) · thing · that · we · own, we · fairly · decide · (thing) · scarce · thing, and · we · decide · together, in · small · circle · that · every · person · can · join · and · every · person · can · leave
>
> Concordance is a way for people to look after each other: sharing what we have, governing fairly what is scarce, and deciding together, in small circles anyone may join and anyone may leave.

**3. Love**

> *Ti lapukola, pan tolo kanu tun yali.*
>
> in · Charter, every · word · try · do · love
>
> Everything here is an attempt to practice love.

**4. What we hope for**

> *Mini inan o telu li pan hu nika pan hu, li sutu wi imo, e li tan po lo, ne toka.*
>
> we · hope · (thing) · world · that · every · person · notice · every · person, that · harm · later · mend, and · that · good · thing · give, not · sell
>
> We hope for a world where no one goes unnoticed, conflicts end in repair, and the best things in life are given rather than sold.

The Selewa says "everyone notices everyone" where the English says "no one goes unnoticed." The language leans toward saying what is, rather than what is not.

**5. The seven principles**

| Selewa | Word by word | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awa pe pan | open · to · all | Open to all |
| Kulu tenu | circle · decide | Local rules |
| Lipa lo | free · give | Freely given |
| Na ena | need · first | Need first |
| Mini hon | we · own | Our own |
| Toto wa | equal · together | Equals together |
| Kon pe wi huwa | keep · for · later · people | Held in trust |

**6. The heart of it**

> *Mi lo, mi na.*
>
> I · give, I · need
>
> I give, and I need. Everyone is both.

## How the seed grows

**Compose first, coin last.** A new root is coined only when a compound can't say the idea. Any member may propose one as a signed `word.coined` record, with its meaning, examples and why it's needed. It becomes standard when circles take it into use (`word.adopted`), the same way the Charter changes.

**Words from every tradition.** Commons words such as *ubuntu*, *minga*, *dugnad* and *langar* can enter as roots, reshaped to the sound rules, with the culture they came from credited in the record.

**The meaning layer.** Each root will be defined in the semantic primes, so every word reduces to meanings all languages share. In the app, a root is identified by the hash of its record, not by its spelling, so scripts and voices can vary while the meaning stays exact.

**One change from the first sketch.** The earlier sketch used *mi* for both "person" and "I." The seed separates them: *mi* is "I," so *Mi lo, mi na* stays as it was, and *hu* is "person." "Those who share the fire" becomes *suhu*.

**Next steps**

1. **Review from many host languages.** Native speakers of Mandarin, Arabic, Swahili, Spanish, Hindi and others read the seed and flag roots that collide with rude or confusing words in their language, or sounds that are hard for them. Where reviews disagree, the seed changes.
2. **Listening tests.** Speakers of many mother tongues hear the roots and say which sound good and which are hard to tell apart.
3. **A native script.** Shapes that show how each sound is made, easy to write by hand.
4. **Define the roots in primes,** starting with the Charter's acts.
5. **Compile one accord.** Write the Work Day accord in precise Selewa and generate its provisions: the first Selewa milestone in the design.
6. **The Selewa accord.** This folder, `accords/selewa/`, adopted like any other accord.
