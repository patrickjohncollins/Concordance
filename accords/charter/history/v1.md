# Concordance Charter, version 1

The full text before simplification, kept as part of the charter's history.

## Introduction

*Concordance* (from the Latin for "hearts together") is a way for people to look after each other: sharing what they have, governing fairly what is scarce, and deciding together, in small circles that anyone may join and anyone may leave.

Much of what makes a life secure comes from other people: the neighbor who checks on you in a crisis, the friend who takes you to the doctor, the stranger who fixes your bike and shows you how. That care is everywhere, but it is fragile. It depends on luck, on who you know, and on whether anyone notices. This charter is how we make it dependable without making it cold.

It describes the spirit we hope to live by, and the few agreements that help when things get hard. Every circle is free to shape it to fit its people and place.

Anyone can begin: find a few people and look out for each other.

## The world we hope to grow

Everything here is an attempt to practice love: to share, to respect each other's freedom, and to care for one another.

We hope for a more beautiful world, and we believe it grows from the small places where we live.

It is a world where no one disappears without being noticed, and shared places are tended with care. Conflicts end in repair, and some of the best things in life are given rather than sold.

This charter can only make room for that world. The beauty comes from what we do, again and again, until it becomes ordinary.

## Our seven principles

1. **Open to all.** Anyone can join, and anyone can leave with their identity and history. Our circle is for each other, never against anyone, and strangers are welcome.
2. **Local rules.** We make our own rules, and everyone affected has a say.
3. **Give what you can.** What passes between us is given freely and received with thanks.
4. **Care for the vulnerable.** We give extra care to those most at risk: the old, the ill, the young and the alone.
5. **Own your information.** Each of us holds our own data. Our network belongs to its members.
6. **Federate, don't centralize.** We cooperate with other circles as equals.
7. **Hold in trust.** Land and shared resources are held for the community and never sold for private gain.

## How we live it

- **We answer.** When someone asks for help, they hear back soon, even if the answer is no.
- **We notice.** When someone drifts away, one of us reaches out.
- **We stand by each other in illness and loss,** and care for those left behind.
- **We work together, and we share meals.**
- **We disagree well.** We criticize ideas, not people.
- **We thank generously, vouch honestly, and give feedback kindly and in private.** Everyone's gifts count.
- **We keep a rhythm.** We meet often enough to know each other, in person when we can.
- **We share the work.** Roles pass around, so no one carries too much and no one is indispensable.
- **We rest, and we celebrate.** No one gives so much they burn out, and we mark good times together.
- **We pass it on.** We teach what we've learned to newcomers and to the young, so it outlasts us.
- **We stay small enough to know each other.** When we no longer know each other by name, it's time to seed a new circle.

## How we decide

- **We begin by listening to everyone.** Each person has a chance to speak before we decide.
- **Everyday matters:** anyone can suggest something, and it goes ahead unless someone raises a concern. We don't wait for perfect: if it's safe to try, we try it and learn as we go.
- **Bigger changes,** including to this charter, need agreement that everyone taking part can live with.
- **We think of those who come after us** when we decide anything that will last.
- **Scarce things** are shared by need first, then by turns. We agree how before we need to.

## When things go wrong

- **We repair harm together rather than blame.** We respond gently first: a word, then a conversation. Asking someone to leave is a last resort, after time to reflect and a chance to put things right.
- **Disagreements** are heard by people both sides trust, who aren't involved.
- **Safety comes first.** Any concern about a child or vulnerable adult goes straight to the person we've chosen to look after safety, and to the authorities where the law requires.

## Our tools

We use open tools that no one owns, so our records belong to us and can't be switched off by anyone else. Members may host a hearth, a small device at home that keeps the circle's records safe and shared. Members can also share their computers' spare power with the circle. What we share in private stays private, even from our tools.

Our tools serve the circle. Anyone who can't or won't use them can still take part, and someone will record their agreement for them. If our tools ever fail us, the circle carries on: we can meet, decide and care for each other without them.

## Joining

To join, introduce yourself, and a member who has met you welcomes you in. Offer one thing you can give, however small, and sign this charter digitally with a key only you hold.

We revisit this charter together whenever it stops feeling right, and regularly even when it does. Anyone who can't accept a change is free to leave, or to start a new circle with the version they prefer.
